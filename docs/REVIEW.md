## Proof correspondence and checks

* **Complete graphs.** Section 2 applies induction after separating values below the mean from those at or above it. The key extra inequality for 0<p<1 is Proposition 6, whose proof uses concavity, Jensen and Hölder, with endpoint estimates in Lemmas 7 and 8. The article invokes prior work for the three-vertex base, a last-pivot case and p≥1. A proof of Proposition 6 alone therefore does not establish Theorem 1. For p≥1 one can verify the original Jensen argument row by row. At vertex i, every positive outgoing difference of maximal values is at most the sum of the positive outgoing differences of the input divided by n. Jensen on the n−1 other vertices gives the factor ((n−1)/n)^p. Summing positive outgoing differences accounts for each unordered pair once.
* **Four vertices.** The six bits in the finite classification correspond successively to 01, 02, 03, 12, 13, 23. The path, cycle, paw, diamond, star and complete graph then have masks 41, 45, 39, 61, 7 and 63. In a disconnected case a nonconstant label is constant across every edge, hence constant along every walk. In a connected case a bijection preserves adjacency in both directions. This makes the coverage step independent of the Mathematica list. For the real inequalities, upper bounds over all inputs and actual nonconstant equality witnesses are separate obligations.
* **Large constants.** In Section 5 the recursion starts with levels 1 and 2; the derived relation that the next level equals twice the current one plus one starts at index 1. Fix m>p and let the branching factor k grow. A ball centered at the selected branching child at level d, with radius d, reaches at most k vertices per level. If a path diverges at level t, its endpoint at depth h is inside this ball only when h≤2t, before the next branching. The ball at the child one step lower and just large enough to reach the root also reaches the final cluster of at least k² leaves. The edge multiplicity k^m and the resulting exponent m/p−1>0 are essential. The root has only one neighbor, so the point mass has variation one for each p>0.

### Independent check of the scalar coefficients in Proposition 6

I checked the coefficient estimate in `PaperHolder.lean` against §2. With k=n−r and l=r−1, let δ=(n/(n−1))^p−1, w=p/(k+l), v=1−kw, and q=1/(1−p). Concave Bernoulli gives 0≤δ≤w≤1. The paper's two Hölder coefficients are A=δ k^(1−p) and B=(1+lδ)/(k+2)^p. For the first one A^q=kδ^q≤kw. For the second, v≥1−p>0 and 1−1/v≤0 imply (1−p)log(v)≥v−1. Since k+2≥3>e, exponentiation gives v((k+2)/v)^p≥v+p=1+lw≥1+lδ. Rearranging yields B^q≤v. Thus A^q+B^q≤kw+v=1. This proves the paper's coefficient inequality on its entire real interval. It replaces the second-derivative and endpoint verification while retaining the same two coefficients. I also checked that the uniform Jensen factor for the upper block is k^(1−p), and that the conjugate exponents 1/p and q in the two-term Hölder step give the required p-th power of the sum. The chord argument for the lower block covers ties and zero total excess.

### Independent numerical cross-check of Theorem 2

On Python 3.12.14 I ran `python3 scripts/crosscheck_four.py`. This independently written check computes distances by Floyd–Warshall and directly forms ball sums on all 38 connected labelled graphs. It uses integer multiples of the averages to avoid rounding. All 9,728 checks of the 3/4 bound passed for inputs with values in {−1,0,1,2}, including inputs of mixed sign and zero-variation inputs. Each graph had a nonconstant equality witness in this sample. The command's output is in `verification/four-crosscheck.txt`. ### Review of the source-tree assembly

In `PaperTree.lean` the source parameter m is t+1. After fixing p and C, the proof chooses m>p, sets D=height(t)+1 and only then chooses k, so D is independent of k. The index set for the distinguished edges has exactly k^m elements. `PaperTreeLeaves.lean` labels the k² comparison leaves by appending two new base-k digits. The leaf labels are distinct for fixed center, retain the center prefix, and the vertical path from its child to a leaf has length at most the child's distance to the root. `TreeDirac.lean` proves the reciprocal ball formula for a point mass and counts the two orientations of the distinct edges before dividing the energy by two. Finally `TreeGrowth.lean` chooses k>2D, giving a difference of reciprocals at least 1/(2Dk). The resulting lower bound on the output energy is k^(m−p)/(2D)^p, so a sufficiently large k gives a strict violation. I checked the order of choices, the powers of k, and the passage from energy through the positive real p-th root. The witness follows the original branching construction, and the proof verifies its connectivity.

## Reading the finished solution

I read the three public theorem bodies in `Solution.lean` in addition to the challenge. The complete-graph proof starts with a signed real input, applies the complete-graph estimate to its absolute values, and transfers the variation bound back by the edgewise reverse triangle inequality. In the four-vertex proof, the isomorphism to a graph on `Fin 4` is composed with the representative isomorphism. The lemma `iso_admissible` proves equivalence in both directions by transporting every test function, so its equality of admissible sets also transports leastness. For the path, I traced the graph reduction to the enumerated ball sets and the signed-edge certificates, and traced the lower bound to a point mass. The other five models use the same upper-bound and nonconstant-witness structure. The large-constant theorem calls the source-tree witness directly, with its original quantifiers and strict inequality.

For a first reading, start with the table of statements in the README. Read `complete_sum_le` for the split at p=1, the path example for how an exact linear certificate is used, and `PaperTree.large_witness` for how the geometric estimates yield Theorem 4. The full build and axiom reports are the mechanical evidence for the remaining generated certificate terms.

The companion module `Constants.lean` returns to the paper's extended-nonnegative constant. `optimalConstant` takes the infimum of the images of finite admissible coefficients in the extended nonnegative reals. This includes an empty family with infimum infinity. The companion proofs use leastness for four vertices and the nonconstant equality witness for complete graphs. The strict lower bound for Theorem 4 bounds every admissible coefficient below by the same witness ratio, which is strictly above C. This addresses the distinction between a failed bound at C and a strict inequality for an infimum.
